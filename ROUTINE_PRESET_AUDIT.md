# Instrument routine audit — 2026-09-21

Scope: every instrument's Fundamentals and Full practice presets, the exercise picker, and the four goal-generated plans. These are editable starting plans, not universal teaching prescriptions. Saved routines retain their settings; there is no reliable template provenance with which to distinguish an old default from a user's customization.

## Evidence and resulting choices

| Area | Teaching evidence | App decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brass breathing and buzzing | [J.D. Handshoe's daily routine](https://jdhandshoe.wordpress.com/daily-routines-for-trombone/) separates breathing from buzzing and explicitly accompanies buzzing with a drone or keyboard. [Jeff Conner's trumpet pedagogy](https://hub.yamaha.com/music-educators/instruments/winds-instruments/trumpet-pedagogy/) emphasizes steady air and comfortable middle-register sound. | All five brass instruments open Tuner with drone for the combined breathing/mouthpiece step. No automatic click; it remains available for counted breathing. The drone is for the pitched portion, not a claim that breath alone has a tunable pitch. |
| Sustained tones | Handshoe uses drones for long-tone lines. [Joshua Gardner's Clarinet Fundamentals, p. 3](https://clarinet.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Clarinet-Fundamentals_v1.7.pdf) distinguishes learning instrument tendencies with a tuner from hearing intervals with a drone. [Terry Ewell's practice guidance](https://www.2reed.net/practicing.html) recommends drones for double-reed long tones, octave tuning, and passages. | Sustained-tone exercises open Tuner with drone and metronome, including bassoon tenor-register work and sustained vowels. Quarter note = 60 in 4/4 is an editable product default for counting notes/rests, not a tempo prescribed by all sources. Flow studies retain their own click and no fixed drone. |
| Instrument setup | [Yamaha's first-year flute guidance](https://hub.yamaha.com/music-educators/instruments/winds-instruments/first-year-flutes-success/) treats headjoint response as its own exercise. Gardner's clarinet material treats voicing and instrument response specifically. | Headjoint, mouthpiece/barrel, sax mouthpiece, and reed-response exercises retain Tuner without automatically imposing the assembled instrument's drone. Bassoon reed response is separated from sustained full-instrument long tones. No universal reed/mouthpiece target pitch is invented. |
| Horn | [James Boldin, International Horn Society](https://www.hornsociety.org/hornzone/642-three-things-you-should-practice-every-day) discusses lip trills, multiple tonguing, and stopped horn alongside daily fundamentals. | Keep horn-specific flexibility, stopped-horn pitch work, and transposition. Correct its combined buzzing warmup from recording to Tuner/drone. |
| Recorder | [American Recorder Society's Vicki Boeckman lessons](https://americanrecorder.org/how_to_play_the_recorder.php) cover foundation, tone, and articulation; its [technique videos](https://mms.americanrecorder.org/video/index.php?org_id=ARSO&tags=20453) include long tones, breath pressure, and phrasing. | Replace generic wind preparation with recorder air/first notes, sustained tone, thumb/finger coordination, and tonguing/slurs. Use comfortable keys and ranges. |
| Bowed strings | [Meghan Faw's intonation guidance](https://www.meghanfaw.com/beginner-violin-lessons/intonation) separates single-note tuner feedback, drones, resonance, and listening to double stops. | Retain camera for bow setup, Tuner/drone for slow intonation, and recording/click for shifting. Clarify single-note checks before sounding double stops together: the app's single-pitch detector does not assess both voices simultaneously. |
| Voice | [Berklee's Playing for Life](https://college.berklee.edu/bt/213/play_for_life.html) recommends gentle warmups and soft clean tones without strain. | Retain camera for body/breath alignment, no fixed drone during sirens, and Tuner/drone/click for comfortable sustained vowels with rests. Register blending/diction retain recording feedback. |
| Piano | [Jerry Kovarsky's Yamaha technique guidance](https://hub.yamaha.com/keyboards/k-how-to/tips-for-practicing-technique-part-1/) recommends separate-hand scale work and metronome practice. | Keep camera for hand alignment, metronome for scales/arpeggios, and recording for voicing/independence. Remove blanket four-octave hands-together language. No pitch-correction drone for a fixed-pitch keyboard. |

Fretted instruments retain their existing posture, chord, picking, groove, and transcription distinctions; no bowed-string drills are inherited. These tool choices are app-design judgments based on the exercise's feedback needs, not claims that a source endorses this app or every preset tempo. Drone notes are editable concert references chosen for the exercise/key, not mandatory instrument tuning notes. Tuner transposition is unchanged.

## Goal-plan follow-up — 2026-09-22

The 54 suggested presets above still use their instrument-specific exercises. I reviewed the four generated goals for all 26 instruments and the general fallback as well. The “Prepare a piece” generator had inserted an unrelated etude after the chosen phrase. It now warms up, establishes tone or easy glides where appropriate, isolates one phrase of the actual piece, and returns to a full-section run-through. This follows [NAfME's practice-strategy guidance](https://nafme.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Practice-Strategies-by-Category.pdf) to put an isolated passage back in context and [Yamaha's passage-practice guidance](https://hub.yamaha.com/pianos/p-how-to/breaking-down-difficult-passages/). The phrase receives the largest time share when the plan has a chosen duration. The reference is optional; presets no longer imply that sheet music or a video was automatically attached.

“Work on tone” now chooses an instrument-appropriate exercise when long tones do not fit: bowed-string intonation with a drone, guitar right-hand attack, bass-guitar groove and note length, ukulele fingerpicking, or piano chord voicing. [Berklee's guitar teaching](https://college.berklee.edu/news/guitar-playing-styles-to-explore-and-master) links right-hand control to tone; [Yamaha's bass technique article](https://hub.yamaha.com/guitars/bass/bass-picking-hand-placement-and-technique/) describes how the plucking hand changes tone; [Ukulele Tricks' fingerpicking lesson](https://ukuleletricks.com/mini-lesson-series/lesson-1/) teaches even volume and tone; [Yamaha's piano technique article](https://hub.yamaha.com/keyboards/k-how-to/tips-for-practicing-technique-part-1/) favors useful, music-connected technique. These are suggested starting points, not a fixed curriculum. Saved user routines are left untouched.

## Verification

`node scripts/verify-routine-preset-tools.mjs` checks all profile IDs plus the generic fallback: no missing/duplicate exercise IDs, complete preset construction, goal-plan tool inheritance, durations, storage round trips, sustained-tone and buzzing tools, and the exceptions above. `node scripts/verify-practice-routines.mjs` covers the existing routine persistence and session behavior. The production build checks TypeScript and bundling. This is preset/configuration validation, not a new on-device microphone or acoustic test.
