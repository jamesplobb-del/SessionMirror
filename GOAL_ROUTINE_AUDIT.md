# Goal-built routine audit — 2026-09-23

Scope: **Build a plan for my goals** for every instrument in the picker (25 named instruments plus **Something else**) and all four goals: daily habit, tone, technique, and piece preparation. This examines the plan a new user receives, including exercise order, tools, wording, and time allocation. A generated plan is an editable starting point. The app does not know the player's level, repertoire, teacher's assignment, physical limits, or preferred style, so it cannot prescribe one universally correct routine.

The sources below support the *skill being suggested*. They do not endorse this app, its default tempo, its drone note, or a precise number of minutes. Those are editable product choices. Concert-pitch drone labels are deliberate; the player still chooses a useful reference for the exercise or key.

## Per-instrument review

| Picker instrument | Research check | Goal-plan decision |

| Soprano recorder | [American Recorder Society lessons](https://americanrecorder.org/how_to_play_the_recorder.php) cover tone and articulation; its [technique guidance](https://americanrecorder.org/docs/AR_fall21_LEARN_articulation.pdf) connects air and tonguing. | Air/first notes, breath-controlled sustained sound, finger/thumb patterns, then tonguing are apt. Use comfortable range rather than assuming flute harmonics. |
| Trumpet | [International Trumpet Guild daily routine](https://www.trumpetguild.org/images/pdf/ITG-NonProCommittee/2023-Montelione.pdf) combines mouthpiece, long tones, flow/slurs, valve work, articulation, and repertoire. | Breath/buzz, long tones, lip slurs, and valve coordination remain instrument-specific. The tone goal gives sustained sound more time than the closing phrase. |
| French horn | [International Horn Society beginner pedagogy](https://www.hornsociety.org/?catid=295%3Anewsletter&id=899%3Akaren-houghton&option=com_content&view=article) puts scales, tonguing, slurs, and trills in core work, while stopped horn and transposition are introduced separately over time. | Broad daily and technique goals choose scales/slurs/articulation instead of automatically inserting stopped horn. Stopped horn stays selectable for players who need it. |
| Trombone | [International Trombone Association routine](https://www.trombone.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/hofacre.pdf) includes Remington-style long tones, slow pulse, slide positions, and clean connections. | Long tones, slide coordination, and legato tonguing remain central; tone practice opens pitch/drone tools, while movement uses time or recording feedback. |
| Euphonium / baritone | [Euphonium-player teaching guidance](https://hub.yamaha.com/music-educators/prof-dev/self-care/its-ok-to-be-bored/) identifies breathing, long tone, lip slur, and tonguing as recurring fundamentals. | These stay in the plan; lyrical work remains the instrument-specific study choice. |
| Tuba | [Yamaha tuba artist Eirik Gjerdevik](https://europe.yamaha.com/en/news/2021/Artist-Insights-feat-Eirik-Gjerdevik.html) emphasizes relaxed long notes, sound, and attacks; [Aaron Tindall's teaching](https://hub.yamaha.com/music-educators/prof-dev/teaching-tips/10-rules-of-play/) covers even tone, fronts, releases, time, and pitch. | Low response, tone, slurs, valves, and articulation are appropriate distinct exercises. |
| Flute | [Yamaha flute pedagogy](https://hub.yamaha.com/music-educators/instruments/winds-instruments/first-year-flutes-success/) separates headjoint response, tone, fingers, and passage work; [NFA practice guidance](https://www.nfaonline.org/docs/default-source/membership-benefits/garrison---current-state-of-practicing.pdf?sfvrsn=dca24da1_2) includes long tones and harmonics. | Headjoint preparation precedes whole-instrument tone, finger patterns, and music. |
| Piccolo | [National Flute Association piccolo guidance](https://www.nfaonline.org/docs/default-source/membership-benefits/raphael---piccolo-perspectives.pdf?sfvrsn=39704406_0) addresses soft response, upper-register intonation, and special fingerings. | Soft-dynamic pitch control and upper-register connection are appropriate; the plan does not simply duplicate flute headjoint work. |
| B♭ clarinet | [International Clarinet Association fundamentals](https://clarinet.org/fundamentals-successful-auditions/) link drones/long tones, register consistency, pulse, and recording. | Mouthpiece/barrel response, long tones, register slurs, and break coordination are coherent. |
| Bass clarinet | [International Clarinet Association bass-clarinet pedagogy](https://clarinet.org/pedagogy-corner-not-like-others-playing-strategies-e-flat-bass-clarinet/) discusses low response, long tones, register changes, and instrument-specific resistance. | Low-register sound and little-finger/break patterns stay separate from soprano-clarinet defaults. |
| Soprano sax | [Yamaha's saxophone comparison](https://hub.yamaha.com/winds/wood/whats-the-difference-between-soprano-alto-tenor-and-baritone-saxophones/) notes soprano's greater intonation sensitivity; [NAfME sax fundamentals](https://nafme.org/event/putting-the-fun-in-saxophone-fundamentals/) cover tone, voicing, technique, and articulation. | Sound/intonation and overtone work fit. | Alto sax | [NAfME saxophone fundamentals](https://nafme.org/event/putting-the-fun-in-saxophone-fundamentals/) cover breath, embouchure, tone, voicing/overtones, fingers, and articulation. | Long tones, overtones, fingers, and articulation remain suitable. |
| Tenor sax | [NAfME saxophone fundamentals](https://nafme.org/event/putting-the-fun-in-saxophone-fundamentals/) apply across the sax family; [Yamaha's comparison](https://hub.yamaha.com/winds/wood/whats-the-difference-between-soprano-alto-tenor-and-baritone-saxophones/) explains the register differences. | Full-range sound and octave/side-key coordination remain distinct from alto's card. |
| Baritone sax | [Yamaha's saxophone comparison](https://hub.yamaha.com/winds/wood/whats-the-difference-between-soprano-alto-tenor-and-baritone-saxophones/) notes bari's larger air demand and low range; [NAfME](https://nafme.org/event/putting-the-fun-in-saxophone-fundamentals/) covers core sax skills. | Low-note response, low long tones, large-key coordination, and groove are sensible defaults. |
| Oboe | [International Double Reed Society practice resources](https://www.idrs.org/MIDI/MIDI_HP.htm) include drone-based long tones, scales, and arpeggios; its [Brod method commentary](https://www.idrs.org/scores/Brod/Commentary/Introduction.html) describes scales, articulation patterns, and progressive studies. | Reed response, long tones, attack/release, and finger patterns remain separate tasks. |

| Violin | [Violin tone interviews](https://stringsmagazine.com/violin-sound-production-tips-on-tone/) tie sound to bow speed, weight, and sounding point; [scale guidance](https://stringsmagazine.com/how-to-embrace-scale-exercises-and-the-progress-they-represent/) discusses intonation with drones. | Tone goal now studies **Long bows & tone** with video/audio feedback; intonation remains separate scale work. |
| Viola | [Viola tone instruction](https://stringsmagazine.com/6-simple-ways-to-improve-your-viola-tone-production/) connects even bows, arm weight, and contact; [viola scale teaching](https://stringsmagazine.com/using-musical-context-to-approach-viola-scale-work/) recommends hearing intervals with a drone. | Long bows and drone-based intonation remain distinct, preventing a tone goal from becoming only a tuner drill. |
| Cello | [Cello bow/arm teaching](https://stringsmagazine.com/play-cello-with-less-effort-in-6-easy-steps/) emphasizes balanced arm weight; [cello thumb-position teaching](https://stringsmagazine.com/rules-of-thumb-position-a-path-toward-mastering-a-dreaded-cello-technique/) treats that position as a staged skill. | Broad tone uses long bows; shifting is the default technical focus. | Double bass | [Bowed-string intonation teaching](https://stringsmagazine.com/14-steps-toward-improved-intonation/) includes bass; [bow contact-point discussion](https://stringsmagazine.com/is-it-possible-to-play-with-a-straight-bow/) explicitly applies across bowed strings, including bass. | Tone uses long bows, scales/positions use pitch reference, and shifting/pizzicato remain distinct options. |
| Guitar | [Berklee's guitar curriculum](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/classical-guitar-101) connects right-hand arpeggios, posture, tone, scales, and repertoire. | Tone plan records right-hand sound as well as keeping a click. Piece work uses an audio phrase, never a bow-arm instruction or automatic polyphonic pitch graph. |
| Bass guitar | [Yamaha's plucking-hand demonstration](https://hub.yamaha.com/guitars/bass/bass-picking-hand-placement-and-technique/) links hand placement to tone; its [bass practice guide](https://hub.yamaha.com/guitars/bass/how-to-create-a-30-minute-bass-practice-routine-that-works/) puts groove and songs in context. | Tone now targets plucking consistency instead of mislabeled groove. Daily and technique goals include groove; piece work refers to a bass line. |
| Ukulele | [Ukulele Tricks' fingerpicking lesson](https://ukuleletricks.com/mini-lesson-series/lesson-1/) emphasizes even volume and tone; its [strumming lesson](https://ukuleletricks.com/5-effective-strumming-patterns-for-beginners/) ties pulse to chord changes. | Tone uses fingerpicking, while daily and technique goals include chord changes and strumming instead of a generic étude. |
| Voice | [NATS vocal warm-up guidance](https://www.nats.org/cgi/page.cgi/5/_articles.html/Pedagogy/The_Five_Best_Vocal_Warm-Up_Exercises) includes gentle glides and semi-occluded exercises; [NATS voice-pedagogy discussion](https://www.nats.org/NATSChat_-_Oct_2_2011.html) places these before open-vowel work. | Sound starts with SOVT/sirens, then sustained vowels and register work. | Piano / keys | [ABRSM piano requirements](https://www.abrsm.org/en-gb/piano?resources_categories_filter=Publications) include scales, arpeggios, sight-reading, and pieces; [Yamaha technique teaching](https://hub.yamaha.com/keyboards/k-how-to/tips-for-practicing-technique-part-1/) addresses separate-hand work and evenness. | Tone uses recorded chord voicing, not a tuner/drone. Technique includes scales, arpeggios, and hand independence rather than treating chords alone as technical practice. |
| Something else / no instrument | No source can define an unspecified instrument. | The generic plan now uses an easy warm-up, sound recording, patterns/coordination, and a musical phrase. It makes no pitch-tracker, drone, bow, reed, or scale-range claim. |

## Cross-plan checks and limits

- Piece preparation ends with an isolated phrase followed by a section run-through, reflecting [NAfME practice strategies](https://nafme.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Practice-Strategies-by-Category.pdf) and [Yamaha's passage practice](https://hub.yamaha.com/pianos/p-how-to/breaking-down-difficult-passages/). No score, recording, or reference is silently attached.
- For tone goals, the sound exercise receives more time than the closing phrase. Fixed BPM values and starter drone notes remain editable. A voice drone has no preset note. Routine drone labels are concert pitch.
- The goal builder does not ask for skill level, style, current piece, or time budget. Advanced catalog items remain available in the review/edit step. That is the defensible boundary for a launch default; an individualized curriculum would need those inputs or a teacher.
